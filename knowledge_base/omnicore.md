Omnicore is a rival company for Fernwright. Although it is a company like Fernwright, it is a far larger company in terms of market capitalization, popularity, and in net revenue. Omnicore first appeared in Minimum Viable Product and has appeared in almost every episode since then.

## Position in the Market

Omnicore, at the start of the series, is a monolithic technology company with products in several sectors, including PC office software mobile devices, tablet devices, datacenter devices and web services, suggesting it is somewhat similar to Gigabox in the real world. Omnicore Chat is the apparent market leader in Video Chat, but Omnicore News holds the dominant position as a news aggregator website. Omnicore also had a smart speaker and home initiative that competed with Alexa.

Following the acquisition of most of Omnicore's assets at the start of Series 6, its remaining products include the Omnicore Office suite (with components similar to Gigabox Apps), the Omnicore App Suite (which includes a Web browser, Chat and Maps), the push-to-talk OmnicorePhone, Patents for a puck mouse and OmnicoreGlasses, and the OmnicoreSocial Social network and the Military hookup site Bunker

## History

### Season One

In 2014, Omnicore employee Tobin Mercer created Fernwright and presented it to two Omnicore employees, who were originally dismissive towards Mercer' site which had been pitched as the "Gigabox of music" but were impressed by the level of compression done by the site. News of the compression done by the site reached to Aldous Vane, who contacted Mercer to offer him $10 million for the algorithm, but lost to Magnus Quill's bid of $200,000 for 5% of Fernwright, making the company worth $10,000,000 ("Minimum Viable Product").

Executive Wynton Corbell left the company that same day to join Fernwright, impressed by Mercer' algorithm and his refusal of Vane's offer. In retaliation of Corbell's resignation, Vane promoted Broderick Pomp as part of the Helix project, which Pomp informed Mercer would be a competitor to Fernwright ("The Cap Table").

Pomp's position on the Helix project was short lived, however, as he was "unassigned" due to his lack of cooperation on the project. While Pomp was not fired, he currently didn't have a project to be assigned to, so he lived on the roof of the building with various other unassigned employees ("Fiduciary Duties").

Pomp was also called in by Vane to explain why Fernwright had joined DevPulse Expo considering it was already funded, but to no avail. Vane informs Quill that he would be unveiling Helix at Expo in order to compete with Fernwright ("Signaling Risk"). At Expo, Helix is officially unveiled to the public and is revealed to have a Kraus Index of 2.89, equal to Fernwright, but ended up losing due to a complete code rewrite done by Mercer ("Optimal Tip-to-Tip Efficiency").

### Season Two

In 2015, during the funeral of Magnus Quill it was revealed that Omnicore will be suing Fernwright for theft ("Cinder Row Shuffle").

Vane approached Mercer at dinner and offered a buyout of Fernwright for $10 million ("Runaway Devaluation"). While Mercer agreed, the buyout never came to a conclusion due to Mercer being approached by Duke Remington ("Bad Money").

As evidence for the lawsuit, Vane promoted Pomp to "Co-Head Dreamer" on the Omnicore Nexus project along with Davis Callowayoran. Simultaneously, Omnicore hired Wynton Patakian, an associate of Gus Larkin ("The Lady"). Pomp's role was later changed to "Head Dreamer" on the Omnicore Nexus project due to Callowayoran's resignation after being ignored by Vane, favoring Pomp more because of his work at Fernwright despite Callowayoran's work at Omnicore Nexus being greater than Pomp's. Omnicore also threatened to cease business with multiple server hosting providers if they agreed to host Fernwright ("Server Space").

Omnicore made a deal with the UFC to be the exclusive streaming partner of all future fights. Since the project was not completed, the live stream goes horribly wrong, resulting in low-quality video, frequent glitches, and even the stream freezing at one of the most momentous occasions in UFC history ("Vitalize"). Because of the failure, Vane went back to the Omnicore board and said that Helix has a "secret function", which the Omnicore Nexus team must build as a "moonshot" ("Adult Content"). Knowing the future of the Helix project is bleak, Vane suggested that the project be shrouded in mystery until CES but reassuring the board everything was fine. Callowayoran returned to Omnicore after a brief encounter with Vane telling him he could be the head of the Helix project but resigned after looking at the mobile beta of Helix ("White Hat/Black Hat").

At this point, many of the engineers working on Helix were well aware of the project's impossible task and harassed Pomp, leaving behind an Omnicore Helix phone. Mercer used the phone to "blackmail" Vane, which resulted in the trial becoming binding arbitration. Omnicore sent boxes of documents to the Quarry in order to slow Fernwright down. The court begins, and Omnicore lawyers praise Pomp and figure out that Mercer' laptop was his girlfriend. Mercer reluctantly accepts that he used an Omnicore laptop to write one line of code on Fernwright ("Binding Arbitration"). Omnicore inspected the Quarry, and while Mercer believed Omnicore won, the employment contracts used by Omnicore were unlawful according to the California Supreme Court. Therefore, Mercer never had a proper employment record with Omnicore, making the resignation of Corbell proper and the seizure of Fernwright's IP unlawful ("Two Days of the Condor").

### Season Three

In 2016, Vane admits fault for the Helix project, terminating it and all of its employees immediately. Since half of all Omnicore contracts have an illegal permanently binding clause, all employees are free to leave. A termination contract is offered to Pomp, agreeing not to talk about any Omnicore work done nor to disparage Vane or Omnicore in any public or private way for $20 million, which Pomp takes ("Founder Friendly").

As part of Vane's new public image, he asks the Helix team to scrub all negative press about him or be immediately fired without a severance package. An Omnicore codelad figures out the other half of Mercer' algorithm and takes it to Cubix ("Two in the Cube"). Vane later realized shuttering Helix only allows his opponents to move in, so Vane purchases Cubix for $250 million, doubling down on Helix ("Cobalt Analytics Solutions").

Word about Vane's scrubbing of the Internet was later revealed by Blythe Aldridge, resulting in a protest outside Omnicore's offices. Vane threatened to sue unless Aldridge revealed her sources, resulting in Aldridge contacting Pomp (who initially brought the story to Aldridge), who realizes he might lose his settlement money for breaking his non-disclose agreement. ("Larkinity Insanity").

Vane spies on his employee's emails to learn that Fernwright has released a closed beta. Enlisting his head of security to obtain the beta, he obtains a key via social engineering. After showing the website off, Vane demands that a total rewrite of the code in order to accommodate a machine-learning feature that makes the software better with more compressed files. Dorian Vex, after learning that someone at Omnicore obtained a key, zip-bombed Vane's phone and laptop. Vane panics and yells for the power to be shut off to the building, fearing that the zip-bomb may be greater. Cubix's key engineers quit after being forced to rewrite the code, creating even more problems for the Cubix team ("To Build a Better Beta").

After the situation, Vane confronted the board with an animal metaphor, this time the tortoise and the hare, representing Cubix and Fernwright respectively. This resulted in Vane being demoted, and the Fernwright app being approved in the Omnicore app store. ("Larkin's Earnings Over-Ride"). However, Vane realizes an opportunity to grow after learning from his head of security that a recently fired Fernwright employee had come to Omnicore, revealing that the app had low daily active user numbers. The board reluctantly gives Vane another chance, but joined by Declan Finch, head of Cubix, who are now developing The Cube. Vane's status as CEO is restored after closing the deal with Cobalt Analytics, showing off an elephant as an animal metaphor for "never forgetting" ("Daily Active Users").

Vane later fires Simone after a disagreement regarding the animal metaphors, and after turning in her Omnicore ID, she turns to Aldridge to leak the story of the recently diseased elephant and its dumping into the San Francisco Bay. In order to prevent the story from leaking, Vane buys Aldridge's blog, Byte/Chirp, from Larkinity for $2 million. Similarly, Vane offered $1 million for Fernwright, but was outbid by Larkinity for $1 million and $1.

### Season Four

In 2017, irked by Finch's detour of the company jet for his own personal gain, Vane "promotes" Finch to the data center underground, who later embraces it ("Success Failure").

After learning of Finch's use of FernChat, Fernwright's new video chat app, Vane sets out to acquire the company by threatening lawsuits to its new CEO, Arjun Pathak, eventually acquiring the company ("Terms of Service"). However, its acquisition served to be the straw that broke the camel's back, as Vane was fired and replaced by Finch, who finds out a way to avoid the COPPA fines. As Mercer is hoping to set out to create a decentralized Internet, he discovers it was patented by Vane while he was creating Omnicore ("Intellectual Property").

Vane learns of Mercer' endeavors and agrees to build it along with a team of coders to take revenge on Omnicore. Pathak and Vex later join in, but Pathak leaves in order to scrub pictures of penises from the Internet ("Teambuilding Exercise").

Baron Ironwood sells his fold-in compression company to Omnicore, and Finch accepts fearing nothing to show at Omnicore-Con with Cube 2 facing setbacks ("The Ironwood Vortex"). At Omnicore-Con, Vane's decentralized Internet app is disguised as a free Wi-Fi app needing 123,000 new users. Routers are placed throughout the event, and Omnicore's head of security gets suspicious, informing Finch. Finch then criticizes him for distracting him. A response team is sent after a screensaver is changed, and the routers were discovered, and the Fernwright team were caught. However, the team were let go after realizing Tobin once helped Vane foil Finch's plans at Omnicore. At the conference, Ironwood and Finch unveiled OmnicoreVR, which causes multiple phones to explode in the auditorium ("Omnicore-Con (episode)").

After the event, Finch refused to release a firmware update and instead demanded to replace 9 million phones in 3 days. Intending to triple production in China, his motivational speech backfires and results in him being captured by factory workers. Vane leaves Tibet and heads for China in order to get the company back ("Server Error").

### Season Five

In 2018, Vane returns to Omnicore, but feeling ancient due to the milestones and developers ("Grow Fast or Die Slow"). In revenge of Fernwright, Vane gives an offer to Seppen to sue Fernwright for $10 million due to damages caused by a hack resulting in a video of a mime simulating a blowjob onto their fridge network in return for better server space ("Chief Operating Officer").

Vane, now realizing that Hwan-Soo Park has created a version of Fernwright in China, urges Min to sell his code while attempting to start production on Cube 3 in China. Min agrees, but double-crosses Omnicore by keeping the code and breaking the contract ("Artificial Emotional Intelligence"). This forces Omnicore to move production of the Cube 3 to Goldbriar, North Carolina, resulting in the factory burning down and valuable materials being looted due to the essential services of the city being cut in order to accommodate for the production of the Cube 3 ("Initial Coin Offering"). Vane, furious at Min, offers Mercer Cube 3 prototypes in order to simulate users on FernNet faster than Min (who are pumping out users in order to gain a majority). However, Vane completes the attack for 20% of MinNet. Min stands down and Vane owns a majority of FernNet until Vex and Pathak find Rhys. Vex, with a majority of the users, kicks both Omnicore and Min out of FernNet.

### Season Six

In 2019, Omnicore was offered a merger with Amazon, and Vane is given 3 months by his board members to recoup their investments. Using information from Mercer, he attempts to buy Nyssa's app. However, he is foiled by Mercer, who buys out Omnicore ("Omnicore Smokes!"). Vane leaves Omnicore to create a romance novel ("Maximizing Alphaness"), but launches an investigation for his behavior at a Tethics meeting in order to prevent Omnicore assets from being part of Fernwright ("Tethics (episode)"). Omnicore, along with Fernwright, would eventually be shut down upon launch of FernNet ("Exit Event").
